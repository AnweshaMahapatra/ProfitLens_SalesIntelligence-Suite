# The Story Behind the Dashboard
## A Sales Intelligence Consulting Case Study

---

### Prologue: When Revenue Hides the Truth

There is a particular kind of business problem that never appears on the balance sheet — the problem of *not knowing*. A company can be generating nearly a billion rupees in annual revenue, selling to dozens of customers across fourteen cities, and still have no clear picture of where it is actually making money.

That was the situation here.

Total revenue: **₹985 million**. Total sales volume: **2 million units**. Numbers that look like success. Numbers that leadership could point to in board meetings and regional reviews. Numbers that told a story — just not the complete one.

The complete story required a different kind of analysis. It required asking not *how much* are we selling, but *to whom*, *where*, *at what margin*, and *what is the trend telling us*? That is the story this dashboard was built to tell.

---

### Chapter 1: The Business Problem

The organisation distributes products across fourteen major Indian markets — from Delhi NCR in the north to Bengaluru in the south, from Mumbai on the west coast to Bhubaneshwar in the east. It sells through 25+ retail customers operating either as Brick & Mortar stores or through E-Commerce channels.

As the network grew, so did the complexity. Regional sales teams managed their own customers. Market-level profitability was never tracked in a consolidated view. The finance team could produce revenue reports, but connecting revenue to cost to margin across customers and geographies required manual effort that took weeks and produced stale results.

Three questions kept surfacing in leadership conversations — and going unanswered:

1. Which markets should we prioritise for growth investment?
2. Which customers are genuinely profitable versus just large?
3. Is our revenue trend improving or deteriorating — and why?

No existing report could answer all three simultaneously, filtered by time period, dynamically. That gap is where this project began.

---

### Chapter 2: What the Data Revealed

**The Revenue Concentration Problem**

The first uncomfortable finding was concentration. Delhi NCR alone accounts for **₹520M — 52.76% of total revenue**. The top three markets (Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Ahmedabad) together represent **81.4% of all revenue**. The remaining eleven markets collectively contribute less than 19%.

This is not a healthy distribution. It means the business's financial performance is effectively tied to the health of a single metro market. If Delhi NCR faces a demand shock, a logistics disruption, or a key customer defection, the impact is catastrophic — not manageable.

The quantity data reinforces this: Delhi NCR also leads sales volume at **0.99M units** — nearly double the next highest market (Mumbai at 0.38M).

**The Profitability Inversion**

Here is where the analysis became genuinely alarming. Total profit margin across the entire ₹985M revenue base is just **₹24.7M — approximately 2.5%**.

But the more important finding is the *distribution* of that margin. When markets are ranked by Profit Margin %, the order is nothing like the revenue rankings:

- **Surat**: 4.9% profit margin — highest in the network, despite contributing only 0.26% of revenue
- **Patna**: 4.1% margin — a tier-2 market that outperforms almost every major market
- **Delhi NCR**: 2.3% — the revenue giant, running at barely breakeven efficiency
- **Kanpur**: –0.5% — loss-making
- **Bengaluru**: **–20.8%** — not a rounding error. Every rupee of revenue generated in Bengaluru costs the business more than it recovers

This is the profitability inversion: the markets receiving the most sales attention and resource allocation are delivering the lowest returns. The markets being underserved are the most efficient.

**The Customer Concentration Risk**

The customer analysis deepened the concern. Electricalsara Stores generates **₹413M in revenue — 41.97% of total**. That single account represents nearly half the business.

But Electricalsara Stores has a **profit margin of just 2.3%** — exactly in line with Delhi NCR, the market where it predominantly operates. The business is structurally dependent on a customer who generates volume but not value.

Compare this to Leader — a smaller account generating ₹17M in revenue — which carries a **7.5% profit margin**. Or Nixon at **4.1%**. Or Electricalslytical at **3.4%**. These accounts are dramatically more profitable per rupee of revenue but receive proportionally less strategic attention because their top-line numbers are smaller.

The customer table in the Profit Analysis page makes this comparison visible for every account simultaneously. It is an uncomfortable read for any sales leader.

**The Revenue Decline Signal**

The Revenue Trend chart tells a story of its own. The peak revenue period appears around mid-2018, where monthly figures approached **₹40M**. By early 2020, the trend had deteriorated to approximately **₹10–15M per month** — a decline of more than 60% from peak.

This is not a seasonal dip. The downward trajectory is sustained across six consecutive quarters. Something structural changed in the business — whether market saturation, competitive pressure, or demand erosion — and the trend line was the first indicator.

The Performance Analysis page, filtered to 2020 (January–June), confirms the gravity of the situation. Revenue for this period stands at just **₹142M** on **350K units**, with a total profit margin of only **₹2.1M**. The profit margin % line in the 2020 trend chart oscillates close to zero, occasionally dipping below it.

---

### Chapter 3: The 2020 Crisis in Detail

The Performance Analysis page functions as a crisis diagnostic tool for 2020. The Profit Target parameter is set at 2% — a minimum viability threshold. Against this benchmark, the picture is stark.

Several customers who were marginally profitable in prior years turned loss-making in 2020:

- **Electricalsquipo Stores**: –11.5% profit margin in 2020
- **Epic Stores**: –4.7% margin despite ₹3M in revenue
- **Expression**: –4.2%
- **Insight**: –1.9%

Meanwhile, the market-level margin picture shifted. Markets like Bhubaneshwar (10.5%) and Hyderabad (6.7%) emerged as margin leaders in 2020 — yet they contribute very little to overall revenue and likely lack the infrastructure for rapid scaling.

Delhi NCR's profit margin dropped to just **0.6%** in 2020. The largest revenue market is now barely covering its costs.

The prior year revenue overlay (Revenue LY) in the 2020 trend chart makes year-over-year deterioration visible month by month. There is no month in the Jan–Jun 2020 window where current revenue exceeds the prior year equivalent.

---

### Chapter 4: The Channel Question

The customer type donut chart surfaces a strategic question the dashboard alone cannot answer: why is E-Commerce at only **24.4% of revenue (₹240M)** when the retail landscape is clearly shifting toward digital?

Brick & Mortar dominates at **₹745M (75.6%)**. This may reflect the organisation's distribution strengths, customer relationships, or geographic coverage. But given the revenue decline trend from 2018 to 2020, the question must be asked: is over-reliance on physical retail channels part of the demand erosion story?

---

### Chapter 5: Strategic Recommendations

Based on what the dashboard reveals, four strategic imperatives emerge:

**1. Diversify away from Delhi NCR dependency**
Actively develop Mumbai (₹150M, 3.2% margin), Ahmedabad (₹130M, 2.2%), and Bhopal (₹60M, 3.9%). These are growth markets with manageable scale and improving margin profiles. The business cannot continue with 52% revenue concentration in a single market.

**2. Restructure the Electricalsara Stores relationship**
At 41.97% of revenue and 2.3% margin, this account needs a commercial renegotiation. Either pricing improves to push margin above 3.5%, or volume is actively rebalanced toward higher-margin accounts. Over-dependence on a single low-margin customer is an existential risk.

**3. Exit or restructure Bengaluru operations immediately**
A –20.8% profit margin is not a performance issue — it is a structural problem. The cost to serve Bengaluru exceeds what can be recovered. Unless a clear path to profitability is identified within two quarters, the market should be deprioritised or exited.

**4. Build the E-Commerce channel deliberately**
At 24.4% of revenue, E-Commerce is underweight. As Brick & Mortar revenue declines, digital channel development is not optional — it is the growth path. Investment in this channel should be a 2020–2021 strategic priority.

---

### Epilogue: What Good Data Does

This dashboard did not discover new facts. The revenue was always ₹985M. Electricalsara Stores was always 41.97% of the business. Bengaluru was always losing money. The data existed — it was simply never assembled into a form that forced a conversation.

That is what good business intelligence does. It does not generate insight from nowhere. It makes the existing reality impossible to ignore.

The numbers were always there. Now, finally, so is the story.
